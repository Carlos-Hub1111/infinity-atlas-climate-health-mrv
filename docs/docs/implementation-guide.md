# Implementation Guide

This guide describes how municipalities, communities, schools and implementing partners can use the InfinityAtlas Climate & Health MRV Toolkit.

## Step 1: Define the local climate-health challenge

Identify the main environmental and climate-related risks affecting children’s health in the territory. These may include:

- waste pollution;
- water contamination risks;
- heat exposure;
- air quality concerns;
- hazardous waste exposure;
- flooding or drainage problems;
- ecosystem degradation;
- lack of local environmental data.

## Step 2: Identify users and stakeholders

Potential users include:

- municipal governments;
- schools;
- local health authorities;
- environmental departments;
- community organizations;
- NGOs;
- public service operators;
- local technical teams.

## Step 3: Collect basic data

Use the data collection templates to register:

- type of risk;
- location;
- affected population;
- proximity to schools or health facilities;
- environmental exposure;
- available evidence;
- responsible institution;
- response actions taken.

## Step 4: Structure indicators

Organize the data into MRV indicators related to:

- exposure;
- vulnerability;
- response;
- mitigation;
- data quality;
- community participation.

## Step 5: Visualize and prioritize

Use the dashboard structure to identify:

- priority risk areas;
- vulnerable communities;
- schools or health facilities exposed to environmental risk;
- urgent response needs;
- long-term planning priorities.

## Step 6: Report and verify

Generate simple reports that can support:

- municipal planning;
- climate-health risk communication;
- public health prevention;
- environmental management;
- donor reporting;
- project monitoring and evaluation.

## Step 7: Improve and scale

After initial testing, improve the data templates, indicators and workflows based on user feedback. The toolkit is designed to be adapted and replicated in other municipalities and vulnerable communities.
